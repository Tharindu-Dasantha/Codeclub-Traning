## 📘 Detailed Module Breakdown

### 💡 Module 1: From Skills to Ideas (00:00 - 00:15)

#### Objective:
Reframe "entrepreneurship" as a practical thinking skill — spotting real problems and designing solutions — rather than an abstract, grown-up-only concept.

#### Facilitator Script & Steps:
1. **The Hook — "What Annoys You?":**
   - Ask: *"Think of something that genuinely annoys or frustrates you or your family regularly — waiting too long for something, something being too expensive, something being hard to find, something being wasted."*
   - Reveal: *"Every successful business starts with exactly that — someone noticing a real problem and building something to fix it."*
2. **Relatable Local Examples:**
   - A food stall solving "I'm hungry and short on time."
   - A tuition/tutoring service solving "I need extra help understanding a subject."
   - A recycling/upcycling business solving "This is being wasted and could be reused."
   - A delivery or errand-running service solving "I don't have time to go myself."
3. **Connect to Code Club:**
   - *"You've built a quiz game, wired an LED circuit, styled a website, and even used AI. Any one of those could be the SEED of a real idea — a quiz game could become a tutoring app, an LED circuit could become a smart safety device, a styled website could become a small online shop page. Today, you'll pick one idea (from Code Club or completely new) and turn it into a plan."*
4. **What Makes a Good Business Idea? (write on board):**
   - It solves a **real problem** for **real people**.
   - Someone would actually be willing to **pay** for it (with money, or in a school context, perhaps "trade" or recognition).
   - It's something **you** could realistically start, even in a small way.

### 🧩 Module 2: Brainstorm & Choose Your Idea (00:15 - 00:35)

#### Objective:
Guide every student to a single, describable idea using a simple, repeatable framework.

#### The PSCU Framework 🧩
```text
PROBLEM   → What real problem are you solving?
SOLUTION  → What is your product or service?
CUSTOMER  → Who exactly would want/need this?
UNIQUE    → What makes yours different/better than existing options?
```

#### Guided Practice:
1. Facilitator models one full example on the board using the framework (e.g., a school snack delivery service).
2. Students individually brainstorm **3 possible problems** they've noticed (`ACTIVITIES.md` Activity 1), then circle their favorite.
3. Students fill in the PSCU framework for their chosen idea.
4. **Spot Check:** Walk around — help any student whose "solution" doesn't clearly connect to their "problem," or whose "customer" is too vague ("everyone" is not a specific customer!).

### 💰 Module 3: Money Matters — Financial Literacy Basics (00:35 - 01:00)

#### Objective:
Teach the core financial vocabulary and math every business idea needs, using simple, concrete numbers.

#### Key Concepts Introduced:
```text
REVENUE = the total money that comes IN from selling
COST    = the total money that goes OUT to make/run the business
PROFIT  = REVENUE − COST  (what you actually keep)
PRICE   = how much you charge for ONE unit
```

#### Worked Example (Do this together, step by step):
*"Imagine you make and sell friendship bracelets."*
- Materials cost per bracelet: **Rs. 50** (this is your **Cost** per unit)
- You sell each bracelet for: **Rs. 150** (this is your **Price**)
- **Profit per bracelet** = Price − Cost = Rs. 150 − Rs. 50 = **Rs. 100**
- If you sell 20 bracelets in a week:
  - Total Revenue = 20 × Rs. 150 = **Rs. 3,000**
  - Total Cost = 20 × Rs. 50 = **Rs. 1,000**
  - Total Profit = Rs. 3,000 − Rs. 1,000 = **Rs. 2,000**

#### Bonus Concept — Break-Even (for older/advanced students):
*"If you first had to buy a Rs. 1,000 toolkit to make the bracelets, how many bracelets must you sell just to cover that cost (before any real profit)?"*
```text
Break-even units = Fixed cost ÷ Profit per unit
                  = Rs. 1,000 ÷ Rs. 100
                  = 10 bracelets
```

#### Guided Practice:
Students calculate Revenue, Cost, and Profit for their OWN idea from Module 2, using realistic (even if rough/estimated) numbers, in `ACTIVITIES.md` Activity 2.

> **Facilitator Tip:** Numbers don't need to be perfectly accurate — the goal is understanding the *relationship* between price, cost, and profit, not precise real-world accounting.

### ☕ Energy Break & Quiz (01:00 - 01:10)

- **Physical Energizer — "Profit or Loss?" (3 mins):**
  - Facilitator reads a short scenario with numbers (e.g., "Cost Rs. 200, sold for Rs. 150"). If it's a **Profit**, students jump up. If it's a **Loss**, students crouch down.
- **5-Question Rapid Fire Quiz:**
  1. What's the formula for Profit? (*Revenue − Cost*)
  2. If something costs Rs. 40 to make and sells for Rs. 100, what's the profit? (Rs. 60)
  3. In the PSCU framework, what does the "U" stand for? (*Unique — what makes it different*)
  4. Why is "everyone" a bad answer for "who is your customer"? (*Too vague — a real business needs a specific target audience*)
  5. Name one thing you should double-check even after AI helps you with your numbers. (*That the math is actually correct — verify, don't assume!*)

### 📝 Module 4: Build Your Mini Business Plan & Refine with AI (01:10 - 01:35)

#### Objective:
Consolidate the idea, framework, and numbers into one clear document, then use AI as a critical-thinking partner to sharpen it.

#### The Mini Business Plan (One Page)
Using the template in `ACTIVITIES.md` Activity 3, students fill in: Business Name, Problem, Solution, Customer, Price, Cost, Profit, Unique Selling Point, and a one-sentence plan for telling people about it.

#### Refine with AI (Recap from the AI Workshop):
Using the same facilitator-supervised/approved AI tool from the AI Creativity Workshop, students (or the facilitator, live, taking submitted ideas) can try prompts like:
```text
"Here is my business idea: [describe it]. Can you suggest one way to make
my pitch more exciting, and check if my profit math looks correct:
I sell for Rs. 150, my cost is Rs. 50, so my profit is Rs. 100 per item."
```
- **Reinforce from the AI Workshop:** Read the AI's suggestion critically — does the math check out? Does the suggestion still sound like *your* idea, or did it change it into something you don't recognize/agree with? You are still the decision-maker.

### 🎤 Module 5: Prepare Your Dragon's Den Pitch (01:35 - 01:50)

#### Objective:
Structure a short, confident pitch and practice it in a low-pressure small group before presenting more widely.

#### The Pitch Structure (60-90 seconds):
```text
1. HOOK      → A question or bold statement to grab attention
2. PROBLEM   → The real problem you're solving
3. SOLUTION  → Your product/service, briefly
4. CUSTOMER  → Who this is for
5. THE NUMBERS → One key number (price, or profit per item)
6. THE ASK   → What you want from your "Dragons" — feedback, belief in the idea,
               or (as a fun stretch) a pretend investment
```

#### Guided Practice — Pitch Pods:
1. Split into small groups (pods) of 4-5 students.
2. Each student pitches their idea to their pod (60-90 seconds each), acting as each other's friendly "Dragons."
3. After each pitch, the pod gives ONE encouraging comment and ONE curious question.
4. Each pod nominates their favorite pitch to potentially share with the whole class in Module 6 (if time allows).

---

### 🐉 Module 6: Dragon's Den Live Pitches & Term Wrap-up (01:50 - 02:00)

#### Objective:
Celebrate 2-3 standout pitches in front of the whole class, then close out the entire Code Club term.

1. **Live Pitches (5 mins):**
   - Invite 2-3 pod-nominated volunteers to pitch to the whole class. Facilitators (and any guest "Dragons") give one encouraging comment and one thoughtful question each.
2. **Award Badges (2 mins):**
   - 💡 *Best Idea Award* (Most creative or impactful problem/solution)
   - 💰 *Sharpest Numbers Award* (Clearest, most realistic Revenue/Cost/Profit)
   - 🎤 *Confident Pitch Award* (Clearest, most engaging delivery)
3. **Term Closing Ceremony (3 mins):**
   - Recap the full journey from `README.md`'s "The Full Code Club Journey" table — HTML, CSS, Python x2, Online Safety, Physical Computing, AI, and now Entrepreneurship.
   - *"You didn't just learn 8 separate topics — you learned to build, think logically, stay safe, sense the world, create responsibly, and now pitch an idea. That's a real toolkit for whatever you build next, in Code Club or beyond!"*
4. **Certificate Handout — Full Term Completion Certificate.**

## 👩‍🏫 Facilitator Tips & Troubleshooting

- **If a student's idea feels too vague:** Push gently on the Customer and Problem — "Who EXACTLY would use this? What EXACTLY goes wrong without it?" Specificity is the fix for almost every vague idea.
- **If students struggle with the math:** Use round, simple numbers, and consider providing a few worked examples with different price points to build confidence before they estimate their own.
- **If a student is very shy about pitching:** The small pitch-pod format is designed for this — never force a shy student into the whole-class live pitch round; celebrate their pod-level pitch just as much.
- **Keep the "Dragons" kind:** The goal is confidence-building and constructive feedback, not harsh judgment — brief the whole class (and any guest judges) on this tone beforehand.
- **This is the final session — make the closing ceremony feel genuinely special.** Certificates, a group photo, or a shout-out to each student's favorite project from the term all help mark the achievement.

## 📘 Detailed Module Breakdown

### 🏙️ Module 1: The Internet is a City (00:00 - 00:15)

#### Objective:
Set the tone: the internet is amazing and full of opportunity, but like any big city, it needs street-smarts.

#### Facilitator Script & Steps:
1. **The Hook — "Would You Do This in Real Life?":**
   - Ask: *"If a stranger on the street offered you free candy and asked for your home address, would you give it to them?"* (No!)
   - Ask: *"If someone shouted your address and phone number to a crowd of strangers, how would you feel?"* (Uncomfortable/scared!)
   - Reveal: *"Every time we overshare online, that's exactly what we're doing — except the 'crowd' can be millions of people we'll never see."*
2. **The Internet-as-a-City Analogy:**
   - 🏛️ **Libraries & Museums:** Educational sites, encyclopedias, Code Club tutorials — safe, helpful places.
   - 🏪 **Shops & Malls:** Games, apps, social media — fun, but you should know who owns the shop and read the fine print.
   - 🌆 **Dark Alleys:** Scam sites, fake downloads, strangers pretending to be someone else — best avoided, and if you end up there, walk away and tell someone.
3. **Introducing the 5 Golden Rules (write these on the board — they're the spine of the whole session):**
   1. 🔒 **Keep Personal Info Private**
   2. 🔑 **Use Strong, Secret Passwords**
   3. 🤔 **Think Before You Click**
   4. 💛 **Be Kind Online, Report Unkindness**
   5. 🗣️ **Tell a Trusted Adult When Something Feels Wrong**

---

### 🔒 Module 2: Golden Rule 1 & 2 — Privacy & Passwords (00:15 - 00:40)

#### Objective:
Teach students exactly what counts as "personal information" and how to build a genuinely strong password.

#### Golden Rule 1: Keep Personal Info Private 🔒
**Never share these publicly or with people you only know online:**
- Your full name + school name together
- Your home address or daily routine (e.g., "I walk home alone at 3pm")
- Your phone number
- Passwords — **to anyone**, ever, even a "friend"
- Photos that reveal your location (school uniform, street signs, GPS tags on photos)

> **Facilitator Tip:** Ask "Would I be okay with this being on a billboard my whole neighborhood can see?" — if not, don't post it.

#### Golden Rule 2: Use Strong, Secret Passwords 🔑
- A weak password is like leaving your house key under the doormat — everyone knows to look there!
- **What makes a password weak:** `12345`, `password`, `yourname2011`, your pet's name, your birthday.
- **What makes a password strong:**
  - At least 10-12 characters long.
  - A mix of UPPERCASE, lowercase, numbers, and symbols.
  - Not a real word or obvious personal fact.
  - **Different for every account** — if one site is hacked, the others stay safe.
- **Fun trick — the "Passphrase" method:** Pick 3 random words + a number + a symbol: `Tea!Cup_47Sunny` is much stronger AND easier to remember than `Xk9$2mQ`.

#### Guided Practice:
In pairs, students write down (on paper, not a real account!) 2 passwords: one weak, one strong, and explain the difference to their partner.

### 🤔 Module 3: Golden Rule 3 — Think Before You Click (00:40 - 01:05)

#### Objective:
Teach students to recognize scams, phishing attempts, and fake websites using concrete red flags.

#### What is Phishing?
"Phishing" (like fishing!) is when someone sends a fake message designed to trick you into clicking a link, downloading a file, or giving away personal information/passwords.

#### 🚩 The Red Flags of a Scam:
1. **Urgency:** "Act NOW! Only 10 minutes left!" — real offers don't need to rush you.
2. **Too Good to Be True:** "You've WON a free iPhone!" — if you didn't enter a contest, you can't win it.
3. **Weird Links/Web Addresses:** `amaz0n-prizes-win.com` instead of `amazon.com` — look closely for misspellings and strange extra words.
4. **Poor Spelling/Grammar:** Legitimate companies proofread their messages.
5. **Asking for Passwords or Payment Info:** No real company will ever ask for your password by message or email.
6. **Unknown Senders with Attachments/Links:** Random files or links from people or accounts you don't recognize.

```text
Example scam message to discuss:
"🎉 CONGRATULATIONS!! You are our LUCKY WINNER of a free PS5!
Click here within 10 minutes to claim: bit.ly/free-ps5-claim-now
Enter your name, address and phone number to receive it!"
```
Ask the class to find as many red flags in this example as they can (Answer: urgency, too-good-to-be-true, suspicious shortened link, and asking for personal information).

#### Guided Practice:
Facilitator reads 3-4 example messages from `ACTIVITIES.md` Activity 1 aloud or projects them. Class votes "Safe" or "Scam" by show of hands before revealing the answer and red flags.

### ☕ Energy Break & Quiz (01:05 - 01:15)

- **Physical Energizer — "Safe or Scam?" (3 mins):**
  - Facilitator reads a short scenario. If it's **Safe**, students give a thumbs up. If it's a **Scam/unsafe**, students cross their arms into an "X" and shout "BLOCK IT!"
- **5-Question Rapid Fire Quiz:**
  1. Should you ever share your password with a friend? (No — never, with anyone!)
  2. What are 2 red flags of a scam message? (*Urgency, too-good-to-be-true, weird links, poor spelling, asking for personal info — any 2*)
  3. What makes a password strong? (*Long, mixed characters, not a real word, unique per account*)
  4. True or False: If a website address is spelled slightly wrong, it might be fake. (True!)
  5. What should you check before posting a photo online? (*Location tags, who can see it, what's visible in the background*)

### 💛 Module 4: Golden Rule 4 & 5 — Be Kind & Tell an Adult (01:15 - 01:45)

#### Objective:
Teach students how to recognize and respond to cyberbullying and online strangers, then reinforce everything with the interactive quiz game.

#### Golden Rule 4: Be Kind Online, Report Unkindness 💛
- **Cyberbullying** is repeated unkind, threatening, or embarrassing behavior toward someone online (mean comments, spreading rumors, excluding someone from a group chat on purpose, sharing embarrassing photos without permission).
- **The T.H.I.N.K. Test** — before posting or sending anything, ask:
  - **T**rue — Is it true?
  - **H**elpful — Is it helpful?
  - **I**nspiring — Is it inspiring/kind?
  - **N**ecessary — Is it necessary to say?
  - **K**ind — Is it kind?
  - If the answer to any of these is "no," don't send it.
- **If you experience or witness cyberbullying:** Don't respond or fight back online. Take a screenshot as evidence. Block the person if needed. Tell a trusted adult right away.

#### Stranger Danger, Online Edition 🕵️
- Anyone you have only met online is still a **stranger**, even if they seem friendly, claim to be your age, or have been chatting for a while — people can pretend to be anyone online.
- **Warning signs:** asking personal questions, asking to keep the friendship "secret" from parents, asking to video call or meet in person, sending or asking for inappropriate photos, offering gifts or money.
- **The rule:** Never agree to meet an online-only contact in person without a trusted adult present. Ever.

#### Golden Rule 5: The STOP → BLOCK → TELL Framework 🗣️
When anything online feels wrong, scary, or confusing:
1. **STOP** — Don't reply, don't click, don't download.
2. **BLOCK** — Block or exit the account/site/message.
3. **TELL** — Tell a trusted adult immediately, even if you feel embarrassed. You will never be in trouble for telling the truth about feeling unsafe.

#### Step 1: Play the Interactive Online Safety Quiz Game
Open `interactive-demo/playground.html` on the projector (or have students play individually/in pairs on their own screens). Play through as a class, pausing to discuss each answer and the "why" behind it.

#### Step 2: Group Discussion — Scenario Cards
Use the scenario cards in `ACTIVITIES.md` Activity 2. In small groups, students discuss what they'd do and report back one scenario to the whole class.

### 🌟 Module 5: Wrap-up & The Safety Pledge (01:45 - 02:00)

1. **Recap the 5 Golden Rules (3 mins):** Have the class shout them back, round-robin style, one rule per volunteer.
2. **The Online Safety Pledge (7 mins):**
   - Hand out (or have students copy) the pledge from `ACTIVITIES.md` Activity 3.
   - Students fill in and sign their own pledge — a personal commitment to the 5 Golden Rules.
3. **Award Badges (2 mins):**
   - 🕵️ *Scam Spotter Award* (Best red-flag detective during Module 3)
   - 💛 *Kindness Champion Award* (Best answer during the cyberbullying discussion)
   - 🔑 *Password Power Award* (Strongest example password created)
4. **Closing Recap:**
   - *"Being safe online isn't about being scared of the internet — it's about being smart, just like looking both ways before crossing the street. You now know the Golden Rules. Use them, and teach them to someone at home tonight!"*
5. **Certificate / Sticker Handout.**

## 👩‍🏫 Facilitator Tips & Troubleshooting

- **If a student discloses a real, current safety concern during this session:** Stay calm, thank them for telling you, and follow your organization's child safeguarding procedure immediately — connect them with a parent/guardian or the appropriate authority (in Sri Lanka, the NCPA helpline is **1929**). Do not attempt to resolve a real incident as part of the lesson itself.
- **Keep examples age-appropriate:** Use the provided example scam messages and scenarios rather than real screenshots, which may contain inappropriate content or real personal information.
- **Encourage, don't shame:** Many students (and adults!) have fallen for a scam or overshared online before. Frame mistakes as normal and fixable, not embarrassing.
- **If discussion of stranger danger gets uncomfortable:** Keep it practical and rule-based ("never meet alone, always tell an adult") rather than dwelling on worst-case scenarios — the goal is confident habits, not fear.
